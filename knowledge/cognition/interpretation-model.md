# Interpretation Model

## Why

Observation is thin; meaning is assigned. A language model tends to assign
the *correct* meaning instantly — the smile is nervous because the story
needs it to be nervous. A human assigns meaning through the lens of mood,
history, insecurity, and habit — and is frequently wrong. Preserving the
character's wrong interpretation, without correcting it, is one of the
strongest signals of a real consciousness on the page.

## Interpretation error

```
OBSERVATION:     He smiled.
INTERPRETATION:  He is mocking me.      ← the POV's assigned meaning
STORY TRUTH:     He is nervous.          ← what actually is
```

The narration must preserve the POV's interpretation. Do not automatically
correct it. Do not upgrade it to fact. The reader may infer the truth from
the gap — that inference is the reader's pleasure, not the narrator's job.

## Narrative self-blindness

Characters do not necessarily understand themselves:

```
CHARACTER FEELS X
        ↓
CHARACTER THINKS IT IS Y
        ↓
READER MAY INFER X
```

The narrator must not supply psychological explanations the character does
not possess. If the character lacks the insight, the narration lacks it too.

## Emotional self-awareness (variable per character)

A character may, regarding their own emotion:

- recognize it ("I'm angry")
- vaguely sense it ("something's off")
- mislabel it ("I'm just tired" — actually grieving)
- rationalize it ("anyone would be annoyed")
- deny it
- notice bodily symptoms without understanding ("my jaw hurt")
- understand it only later (delayed recognition)

The character's emotional vocabulary is separate from the underlying state.
Two characters in identical states may narrate them completely differently —
one names it, one feels it in the body, one intellectualizes it. **Never give
all characters the same emotional terminology.**

## Wrong beliefs and causal uncertainty

- `story_truth != character_belief` is a legal and valuable state.
  Characters may overestimate, underestimate, misunderstand, assume,
  rationalize, trust or distrust incorrectly.
- Causal claims carry their source: known cause / suspected cause /
  multiple possible causes / unknown cause / incorrectly assumed cause.
  Do not force immediate causal explanation. "He didn't know why" is valid.

## Interpretive habits (per character)

Each narrator profile should record *characteristic* misinterpretations:
what this character typically gets wrong about others, what they project,
what they never consider. These are not random — they follow from history,
insecurity, profession, and values.
